Set up Sotto Voce

Sotto Voce types through its own keyboard. Open the app once and it walks you through three steps.

  1. Allow the microphone. Dictation needs it, and your iPhone asks once.
  2. Add the keyboard. The app opens its page in the Settings app: tap Keyboards, turn on Sotto Voce, then turn on Allow Full Access. iOS shows a general warning about Full Access; the next section explains exactly what Sotto Voce uses it for. (You can also add it from Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Add New Keyboard…)
  3. Learn your voice. Back in the app, switch to the Sotto Voce keyboard, tap Start and read the sentence on screen out loud, then tap the listening screen. Sotto Voce learns what your voice sounds like, so it can leave out other people talking nearby.

The app’s Help screen has a short video for each step.

Why Full Access is needed

In short: iOS doesn’t let keyboards use the microphone, so the app listens and the keyboard types what it heard. Full Access is what lets the two share that text.

When you tap Start, the keyboard asks the Sotto Voce app to listen and transcribe. The app turns your speech into text on your iPhone and passes the words back to the keyboard through a small shared storage area on the device (an “App Group”). iOS only lets a keyboard read and write that shared area once Full Access is on.

That is all it is used for. The keyboard makes no network requests of any kind, and nothing you type or say is sent anywhere. There is no analytics, no crash-reporting service and no account. See the Privacy Policy for the details.

Dictate in any app

  1. Tap into any text field — a message, an email, a note, a search box.
  2. Touch and hold the globe key and choose Sotto Voce to switch to the Sotto Voce keyboard.
  3. Tap Start and talk. The wave moves with your voice, and your words are typed as you speak.
  4. Tap anywhere on the listening screen to finish; touch and hold it to move the cursor. Clear wipes the line, so you can say it again.

The first time you dictate in a session, Sotto Voce opens briefly to start the microphone — that’s an iOS requirement, not a choice. If you aren’t taken back automatically, swipe right along the bottom edge of the screen, or tap the back link at the top-left, to return to the app you were typing in. After that it keeps running, and Start works in place, with no more switching.

When you’d rather type, the keys are a full keyboard with autocorrect, and emoji you can search by name.

Turning the microphone off

While Sotto Voce is holding the microphone, the Dynamic Island shows it. To turn the microphone off, press and hold the Dynamic Island, then tap the power button. You can also open Sotto Voce and tap Turn off the microphone. If Live Activities are off for Sotto Voce, there is nothing to show the microphone is on, so the app lets go of it after each dictation instead.

Troubleshooting

Sotto Voce isn’t in my list of keyboards

Adding the app doesn’t add the keyboard by itself. Go to Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Add New Keyboard… and choose Sotto Voce. If you’ve added it but the globe key skips it, check it’s still listed under Keyboards — removing and re-adding it is harmless.

The keyboard says it needs Full Access, or Start does nothing

Go to Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Sotto Voce and make sure Allow Full Access is on. Without it the keyboard can’t receive the text the app transcribes. (Why it’s needed.)

Also check that Sotto Voce has microphone permission, in Settings › Apps › Sotto Voce.

Do I have to switch apps every time I dictate?

Only once per session. The first time, Sotto Voce opens briefly to claim the microphone, which iOS requires. After that it stays running in the background, and Start begins and ends dictation in place, in whatever app you’re typing in.

The exception is when Live Activities are off for Sotto Voce: then it lets go of the microphone after each dictation, so the app opens each time. Turn them on in Settings › Apps › Sotto Voce.

It’s missing my words, or typing what other people say

Sotto Voce learns your voice from the sentence you read during setup, and keeps refining it as you dictate. If it starts leaving out your words, or letting other voices in, open Sotto Voce, go to Settings and tap Relearn my voice, then read the sentence again somewhere quiet. Your current voice profile stays until the new one has been learned.

Does it work offline? What does the app download?

Yes, once its models are on your iPhone. Speech is turned into text by Apple’s speech recogniser, on the device; if your iPhone doesn’t already have Apple’s English speech files, iOS downloads them from Apple. Sotto Voce also uses two small open models that run on your iPhone — one notices when someone is speaking, the other recognises your voice — and downloads them once from Hugging Face, a static file host, the first time they’re needed. After that it works offline.

These downloads are one-way: none of your audio or text is sent. They’re the only network requests Sotto Voce makes.

A model download is stuck or failed

Downloads need an internet connection only while they run. Check you’re online, then open Sotto Voce; if it says something went wrong, tap Try again.

Is the text always right?

Treat it as a first draft. Dictation is turned into text on your device and can contain mistakes — names and numbers are the usual trouble spots. Check anything important before you send it.

Where are my dictations kept?

Your words go into the app you’re typing in. Sotto Voce saves no audio.

While Keep recent dictations is on, which it is unless you turn it off, the app also keeps your last 10 dictations in its own storage on your iPhone, so you can find them again on its Home screen. Turn it off, or clear the list, in the app’s Settings. There is no server copy, and deleting the app removes everything at once.

Free, Pro and purchases

How much can I dictate for free?

Up to 1,000 words a week through the keyboard, with no account. The allowance resets every week.

What does Pro add, and what does it cost?

Pro removes the weekly word limit — everything else stays the same. It’s billed monthly, yearly (with the first 7 days free), or once for lifetime. Prices are shown in the app, in your local currency, before you buy, and purchases are charged to your Apple Account.

I bought Pro but the app doesn’t show it (restore purchases)

Open Sotto Voce, go to Settings and tap Restore purchases. Make sure you’re signed in with the same Apple Account you bought Pro with. There’s no Sotto Voce account to sign in to — your purchase is tied to your Apple Account.

How do I cancel or change my subscription?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple. On your iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, and choose Sotto Voce. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date to avoid being charged for the next period; you keep Pro until the end of the period you’ve paid for.

Refunds are handled by Apple too — request one at reportaproblem.apple.com.
