The short version

Everything you say or type in Sotto Voce stays on your iPhone. Speech is turned into text on the device, and no audio is saved. There are no accounts, no servers of ours, and no analytics. Sotto Voce collects no data at all, which is why its App Store privacy label reads “Data Not Collected”.

The only network requests the app makes are one-way downloads of on-device model files, not your data. See Model downloads below.

How dictation works

Sotto Voce turns speech into text with Apple’s speech recognition, running on your device. Before your speech reaches it, two small models, also on your device, check whether someone is speaking and whether it is you, so that other people talking nearby are left out. None of this sends audio or text anywhere, including to Apple’s servers.

Audio is processed as you speak and then discarded. Sotto Voce does not save recordings.

Earlier versions of the app could record and import audio, and make transcripts and summaries of it. That feature has been removed. If you used it, those recordings, transcripts and summaries are deleted from your iPhone the first time you open the current version.

Your voice profile

To leave out other people’s voices, Sotto Voce learns what your voice sounds like. During setup you read one sentence out loud, and the app turns it into a voice profile (sometimes called a voiceprint): a short list of numbers describing the sound of your voice. It is not a recording of what you said. As you dictate, the profile is refined from your own speech.

The voice profile is worked out on your iPhone, kept in the app’s own storage there, and never uploaded or shared. It is used for one thing only: telling your voice apart from other voices while you dictate. Relearn my voice in the app’s Settings replaces it, and deleting the app removes it.

Why the keyboard needs Full Access

iOS keyboard extensions cannot use the microphone directly. Sotto Voce’s keyboard works around that by having the main app claim the microphone and hand transcribed text to the keyboard through a small piece of storage shared between them (an “App Group”), and iOS only allows a keyboard extension to read that shared storage once you grant Full Access.

Full Access is used solely for that local, on-device sharing between the keyboard and the app: letting the keyboard know a dictation session is running, reading back the words it produced, and remembering keyboard preferences such as your recently used emoji. The keyboard makes no network requests of any kind, and Sotto Voce does not use Full Access for analytics or any purpose beyond this.

Model downloads

Two small open models run on your device: one that notices when someone is speaking, and one that recognises your voice. The first time they are needed, the app downloads their files from Hugging Face’s servers over the network. Separately, if your iPhone does not already have Apple’s speech recognition files for your language, iOS downloads them from Apple.

These are one-way downloads of files. No audio, dictated or typed text, voice profile or personal information is uploaded as part of them, and nothing is sent back. Once the files are on your iPhone, dictation works offline.

What Sotto Voce stores

The app saves what it needs to do its job, and nothing else:

  • your voice profile, described above
  • your recent dictations, while Keep recent dictations is on. It is on unless you turn it off, and keeps your last 10, so you can find a dictation again. Turning it off deletes them.
  • a count of how many words you have dictated, used to show your usage and to apply the weekly free allowance: a number, not a copy of what you said
  • downloaded model files
  • your settings, and keyboard preferences such as recently used emoji
  • crash and hang reports that iOS provides to the app, which contain no audio and no text, and which stay on your iPhone. (Separately, if you have chosen in your iPhone’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass crash reports on under its own privacy policy.)

No audio recordings are stored.

All of it is written to private storage on your iPhone. It is not uploaded to any server of ours, and never transmitted to anyone. Nobody, including the developer, can see it. Recent dictations are left out of iPhone backups; the rest is small app data that iOS may include in a backup you make yourself, to iCloud or a computer, under your own Apple Account.

You can clear recent dictations, or relearn your voice, from within the app. Deleting the app removes everything at once. There is no copy anywhere else to request the deletion of.

Purchases

Sotto Voce Pro is bought through the App Store, and Apple processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. Sotto Voce never sees your payment details, and there is no Sotto Voce account to link a purchase to.

What we don’t do

  • No user accounts or sign-in.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • No third-party AI service: nothing you say or type is sent to one.
  • No third-party data sharing: there’s no data collected to share.
  • No recordings, and no copy of your dictations or voice profile on any server of ours.

Children

Sotto Voce is a general-audience app and is not directed at children under 13. Because the app collects no data from any user, it does not knowingly collect data from children either.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you have rights of access, correction, erasure and objection over personal data held about you. For Sotto Voce, those rights are satisfied directly on your device: everything the app stores is on your iPhone, your recent dictations and voice profile can be cleared or replaced in the app, and all of it is destroyed when you delete the app. The developer holds no copy and so has nothing to disclose or erase.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes it will be updated on this page and the date below will change with it. If a change ever materially affects how your information is handled, it will be described here rather than quietly amended.
